How to Win Friends and Influence People

I read How to Win Friends and Influence People by Dale Carnegie. Here are my takeaways.

1. You Can’t Win an Argument

Even if you “win,” you lose. Arguments make the other person defensive and hurt their pride. Avoid arguments altogether. Instead of proving someone wrong, listen, ask questions, and keep your cool. This leads to far better outcomes.

2. Never Tell Someone They’re Wrong

When someone makes a mistake, resist saying, “You’re wrong.” Instead, ask questions that guide them toward realizing it on their own. It turns conflict into cooperation, saving relationships and reputations.

3. Ask Questions Instead of Giving Orders

People respond better when they feel they have a choice. Rather than saying, “Do the dishes,” try, “Could you give me a hand with the dishes?” Simple phrasing changes invite collaboration instead of resistance.

4. Remember Names

A person’s name is, to them, the sweetest sound in any language. Remember it, use it, and watch people instantly warm up to you. It’s a small habit that builds trust.

5. Talk in Terms of Others’ Interests

The fastest way to someone’s heart is by talking about what they care about. Ask questions, show curiosity, and encourage people to share their passions. When you focus on others, connections deepen naturally.
